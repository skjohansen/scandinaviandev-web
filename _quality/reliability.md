## What reliability means

Reliability shows up in four characteristics:

- **Maturity** — the system meets needs for reliability under normal operation
- **Availability** — the system is operational and usable when needed
- **Fault tolerance** — the system operates as intended despite hardware or software faults
- **Recoverability** — data can be restored and the system can return to operation after an interruption

## How reliability improves security

Reliable systems assume things will fail — including being attacked — and
plan for it:

- **Fault tolerance limits the blast radius** — a component under attack degrades instead of taking the product down
- **Recoverability is resilience in practice** — you can restore data and return to operation after an incident, which is what attackers actually try to prevent
- **Availability is security your users can feel** — a product that stays up under stress tells customers, partners and attackers something

We simulate real breaches and failures with your leadership and engineers,
so the gaps in your response are found during rehearsal — not during the
incident.

## Reliability in AI-supported development

AI-supported development needs ground truth to stand on. Assistants can
only be trusted to change code if the system tells them the truth about
what their changes did:

- **Deterministic builds and tests are the safety net for AI changes** — when a suite is reliable, a failing test means the AI's change broke something, not that the suite flaked
- **Flaky tests make AI unsafe at any speed** — if red can mean "maybe", engineers stop trusting the feedback and start shipping blind
- **Clear failures teach faster than silent ones** — when a system fails loudly and observably, both engineers and their AI tools learn from it
- **Recovery paths you have rehearsed make AI-assisted operations trustworthy** — rollback, restore and failover you can actually perform
